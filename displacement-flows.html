Then and now

A 2017 advocacy snapshot set against current UNHCR figures. Millions of people.

  • The 2017 page, compiled by the Transform Defence campaign, reports that the UN counted more than 10,000 deaths in Yemen by August 2016, and cites the Yemen Data Project finding that over a third of Saudi-led air raids in that period hit civilian sites.
  • It also records that the UK licensed over £3.3 billion of arms to Saudi Arabia from March 2015. These are 2015–2016 figures as reported by that campaign and its cited sources, not current data.

Verified reporting

UN agencies, an EU agency, a think tank and news organisations. Links open the originals.

Method and limits. This is a dated snapshot of published figures, not a live data feed. Flows from Türkiye, Lebanon, Jordan, Iraq and Egypt into Syria are derived by applying UNHCR’s shares of returnees (40, 38, 18, 3 and 2 percent, which round to 101) to 1.63 million returnees counted at 30 April 2026. Host-country counts differ by source (for example Lebanon: about 0.9 million per UNHCR, 1.12 million per IOM), and Refugees International counts 4.7 million Syrians outside Syria against UNHCR’s 3.9 million. Yemen’s newly displaced figure (299,335) is IOM DTM data shown on the UNHCR portal at 31 October and was still rising; earlier IOM releases used different baselines (since June or July), so the chart uses only counts measured from 1 August, and the daily pace is derived from them. The Transform Defence page is an advocacy source dated 2017 (updated 2020), so its figures are historical and are used only for the comparison. Syria’s IDP total is UNHCR’s end-2025 figure. The map is a simplified vector drawing for orientation; borders are indicative, not authoritative.
