Research questions

1. Which Gulf state imports the most?
Saudi Arabia (6.8% of global imports, 2021–25), the region’s largest importer since 2011–15. Qatar is second after more than doubling its imports between 2016–20 and 2021–25.
2. How have imports changed?
Middle East imports fell 13% between 2016–20 and 2021–25, driven by lower Saudi, Emirati and Egyptian purchases, while Qatar surged. Saudi’s share slipped from 8.4% (2019–23) to 6.8%.
3. Who are the suppliers?
The USA dominates: 77% of Saudi, 62% of Kuwaiti, 48% of Qatari and 42% of Emirati imports. France, Italy and the UK fill most of the rest for Qatar and others.
4. What events drive procurement?
The 2026 Iran war, with Iranian drone and missile strikes on Gulf states, triggered emergency US sales for air and missile defense. Yemen’s war and Red Sea shipping risks sustain demand and illicit flows.

Strategic implications

Air defense is the priority
2026 approvals concentrate on Patriot, radars and battle-command systems (Kuwait, Qatar), plus low-cost precision rockets (APKWS) for UAE and Qatar, reflecting a shift toward countering drones and missiles.
Dependence on one supplier
With over three-quarters of Saudi imports from the US, spare parts, munitions and repair services (for example Kuwait’s Patriot repair sale) rest on Washington’s licensing decisions.
Speed over oversight
The March sale was cleared on an emergency justification without congressional approval, showing how crises compress review and raise end-use monitoring questions.
Diversion and illicit flows
The 750+ ton interdiction bound for the Houthis (July 2025) shows the embargo is contested at sea. CAAT also flags diversion risk from Gulf re-transfers; the UAE denies the Sudan allegation.
Diversification and autonomy
Qatar’s mix of US, French and Italian systems cuts single-source risk but adds integration and sustainment cost; local industry and shared GCC air defense are the long-term hedge.
Chokepoints stay central
Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb tie arms demand to energy security, so maritime defense and escort capacity stay procurement drivers.
