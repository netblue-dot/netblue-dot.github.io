Executive assessment

The Gulf is the world’s most important energy-export hub. Before the 2026 conflict, the Strait of Hormuz carried about 20.7 million barrels of oil a day in 2024 and 20.9 million in the first half of 2025, roughly 20% of global petroleum liquids consumption (EIA). Asia is the main buyer: China is Saudi Arabia’s largest crude destination. Since the US-Iran war began in February 2026, Hormuz shipping has been severely disrupted, so the 2024 and 2025 figures below are a pre-conflict baseline and not today’s flows.

    Historical energy security timeline

    ANALYST OUTLOOKThe Gulf remains central to global energy security, and Asia remains the principal buyer of its hydrocarbons. The 2026 war has shown how concentrated the system is: Hormuz is still the most important chokepoint, and shipping through it is far below normal while crude exports recover through alternative routes. Asian importers, especially China, India, Japan and South Korea, carry the most exposure. Security of the routes linking the Persian Gulf, the Red Sea and the Mediterranean remains the main driver of market stability. This is an analyst judgment drawn from the cited sources.
